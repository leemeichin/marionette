The MVP loop

The canonical small plan: ship the smallest testable slice, iterate up to three times on the evidence, and force the pivot-or-kill conversation instead of letting attempt four happen by inertia. The human holds both the launch gate and the kill switch.

Where this shape of plan earns its keep

  • Agent-run projects with real stakes. The plan is the agent's operating envelope: it can only take edges that exist, gates compute availability from live variables, and your sign-offs are walker-enforced @human checkpoints — autonomy boundaries as versioned artifacts instead of prompt-soup.
  • Migrations and cutovers. Contingencies ("what if parity drifts?", "what if the freeze window gets pulled?") become phases with exits, not hopes. The compiler proves every fallback path actually leads somewhere.
  • Iteration with a budget. "Try up to N times, then escalate" is the native loop shape: a monotonic counter, a gated exit, and a human decision when the budget runs dry.
  • Decision records that write themselves. Every taken edge logs actor, timestamp and rationale; render shows the taken path; summarize is the status report. The plan file and its state are the audit trail.
  • Anything you'd sketch as a flowchart and then ignore. Here the flowchart compiles, drifts loudly (exit 3) when reality diverges, and migrates (state rebind) instead of rotting.
